ITER retains its original CAD coordinates (about 10 units high), rather than
a unit-domain rescaling. Its thickness and clearance are arc-weighted
**Euclidean contour distances**; the other geometries use **torsion-flow
arclength**. The CSV labels this distinction. Widths measure separation of
the lower and upper potential thresholds, not the value of delta itself.

The window is logistic with epsilon/delta=0.08. Delta is 0.001 for star,
Ugly Pacman and horseshoe, and 0.008 for ITER. All final fields use affine
triangles, CG2 potential, P4 torsion and quadrature degree 24. The acceptance
threshold is 1e-11 in the normalized stiffness-dual PDE residual. This is a
discrete equilibrium certificate, not a spatial-convergence study.

## What is certified

Star, Ugly Pacman and horseshoe pass the full Equiband PDE and geometry
guards, including the fixed torsion-flow atlas, unique threshold crossings,
transversality, inner-level margin and closed middle contour. Their D_T
ranges are 0.92917–0.95311, 0.93673–0.94985 and 0.93372–0.95591.

ITER passes the same semilinear PDE residual gate, the comparison-principle
check, and independent subdivision-stabilized closed-contour checks at all
three thresholds. It does **not** have a torsion-flow distance/branch
certificate: the recovered CG3 gradient has four critical points. Its status
is explicitly `PDE_AND_CLOSED_CONTOURS_VERIFIED`, and its distance is null.

The ITER attempts also exposed a boundary classification issue: stationary
zero-Dirichlet vertices at sharp corners were counted as extra interior
torsion centers. The correction recognizes only boundary vertices at
coordinate-roundoff tolerance; nearby interior critical points and unknown
cells still fail the guard. The atlas schema is now v4. The nine other fields
were computed with the preceding v3 atlas and retain that recorded provenance.

For ITER, a coarse h=0.06 equilibrium supplied a Newton initial guess for the
h=0.015 mesh. The first fine solve required three iterations, followed by
new fine-mesh solves for the other two midpoints. The first state's mass
changed from 2.578035108 on the coarse mesh to 2.577761193 on the fine mesh
(about 0.0106%). This is a useful initialization check, not an error estimate
or a complete mesh-refinement study.

The threshold-optimizer convergence convention—loss reduction followed by
stabilization—remains documented in the earlier findings report. This catalog
uses fixed-window PDE solves and continuation, so no threshold-loss plateau
is claimed.

## Backend and verification

PyPardiso worked through a serial PETSc Python preconditioner; MUMPS was not
needed. MKL was set and queried at 16 threads. Representative large factors
used about 9–14 CPU seconds per wall second, rather than merely relying on
environment-variable settings.

Twenty-two unit tests passed, covering the direct-solver matrix-update path
and geometry regressions. Small end-to-end checks exercised PDE solving,
portable export, resume, plotting and the contour-only path. With and without
the auxiliary atlas, the disk potential coefficients differed by at most
9.78e-16. The coarse-checkpoint sampler reproduced its source nodal values
to 3.99e-16. All twelve final portable files passed checksum, finiteness,
field-degree, mesh-size, window-parameter and certificate checks.

## Guiding-center handoff

Each state includes a complete portable CG2 potential and frozen window
parameters. Evaluate that potential at DG quadrature points, apply W there,
and project the resulting density. The original tagged meshes are bundled
under the catalog's `inputs/`; the approved Ugly Pacman boundary is unchanged.

The HDG nonlinear-density wrapper, consistent Poisson re-solve, residual
triad and unperturbed evolution remain follow-up work. These saved fields
prepare that preservation experiment; no guiding-center time integration
or irregular-geometry instability study was performed in this campaign.
